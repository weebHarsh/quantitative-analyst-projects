Where profit came from

Numbers: seeded sample run, 6 hours, 15,895 fills. Source: projects/01-post-trade-analytics/examples/sample_report.md

A market maker is a currency booth: buy low, sell high.

The halfway price is the mid. Against the mid, each trade here earns 0.01 (spread capture). Our real run earned about 0.6 basis points per trade (bps, where one bps is 0.01%).

Between trades the booth holds stock, and prices keep moving.

Total profit and loss (PnL) splits into three parts.

The three parts add up to the booth's real worth. A test checks that to nine decimals. Fees are the exchange's cut.

The code is a pipeline: trades and prices in, report out.

A trade's verdict arrives later: did the price move against us?

Customers who know a move is coming trade just before it (adverse selection). Each trade's later check is its markout. The drop from the first bar measures the damage.

Was that skill or luck? The test score says luck.

The annualised Sharpe of 2.62 looks strong, but it stretches six hours of data into a year. The test score is the honest check.

Read the split first, then the markouts, then the luck test.
