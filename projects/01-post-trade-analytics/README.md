# Post-trade analytics engine

A post-trade analytics engine that answers "where did the PnL come from, and was the edge real?" for a market-making strategy. It splits mark-to-market PnL into spread capture, inventory and fees, measures adverse selection with post-fill markouts, and reports risk and performance statistics.

## Headline results

These numbers come from `examples/sample_report.md`. **The data is synthetic** (generated by `src/pta/data/synthetic.py` and replayed through a deliberately simple market maker in `src/pta/strategy/market_maker.py`). The strategy is roughly break-even: the t-statistic of mean bar PnL is 0.16, well under 2. This shows the analytics tooling, not a trading edge.

Session: 6.0 hours, 15,895 fills, 31,667 units traded, notional 3,157,541, marked to a final mid of 101.3150.

| Component | PnL |
|---|---|
| Spread capture (fill price vs mid at fill) | +188.94 |
| Inventory (price move while holding) | -151.95 |
| Fees | -31.58 |
| **Total** | **+5.42** (+0.017 bps of traded notional) |

Markouts (quantity-weighted average, bps of fill price):

|---|---|---|

The 0s row is the spread captured against mid. The decay after it is adverse selection. The report's t-statistic of mean bar PnL is 0.16. The annualised Sharpe of 2.62 scales a few hours of data up to a year and should not be read as evidence of an edge.

## Method

**Attribution identity.** For fill j with side s (+1 buy, -1 sell), quantity q, price px, mid m_j at the fill, and final mid M (from `src/pta/pnl.py`):

```
s*q*(M - px) = s*q*(m_j - px)     spread capture: edge against mid at the fill
             + s*q*(M - m_j)      inventory: price move while holding the fill
```

Summed over fills and net of fees, `total = spread_capture + inventory - fees`. This equals cash + position*M - fees, which `equity_curve` computes without using the decomposition. The tests check that the two agree.

**Markouts.** From `src/pta/markouts.py`: `markout_h = side * (mid(ts + h) - px)`, per unit. Positive means the market moved in our favour after the fill. At h=0 it equals the spread captured against mid; the drop from h=0 to larger h is the adverse-selection cost. Reported averages are quantity-weighted and expressed in bps of fill price.

**As-of mid rule.** The mid used for a timestamp is the last quote at or before that timestamp (`mid_asof` in `src/pta/pnl.py`). It is NaN before the first quote or after the last.

**Fills past the last quote.** A fill whose `ts + h` is past the last quote has no markout (NaN) and is excluded from that horizon, which is why `n` falls at longer horizons. Attribution raises an error if any fill has no mid at or before its timestamp, and `validate` requires every fill to fall inside the market time range.

**Risk statistics.** From `src/pta/risk.py`. Bars are fixed-width time buckets (default 1 minute) of the mark-to-market equity, and each bar's PnL is the change in bar-end equity. The first bar is measured from 0.
- Max drawdown: largest peak-to-trough drop of the bar-end equity.
- VaR and ES: historical, reported as positive loss numbers, at 95% and 99%.
- Sharpe annualised: `mean / std * sqrt(bars_per_day * 252)`, with `bars_per_day = 390` by default. The module docstring says to change both values if your session differs.
- Sortino annualised: mean divided by downside deviation, scaled the same way.
- t-statistic: `mean / std * sqrt(number of bars)`.

## Quickstart

Run from this directory.

```bash
pip install -e .[test]
pytest -q
pta simulate --seed 7 --out out
pta report --fills out/fills.csv --market out/market.csv --out out
```

C++ reference kernel (create `build/` first, since the compile step writes into it):

```bash
mkdir -p build
g++ -O2 -std=c++17 cpp/markout_kernel.cpp -o build/markout_kernel
./build/markout_kernel out/fills_cpp.csv out/market_cpp.csv 0 1000000 5000000
```

The kernel prints `name,value` lines for the attribution totals and `markout,<h_us>,<n>,<qty>,<total>` lines for each horizon. `tests/test_cpp_parity.py` checks it against the Python implementation and skips if `g++` is missing.

`pta analyze --fills ... --market ...` prints attribution, risk and markouts as JSON instead of writing a report.

## Data schemas

Defined in `src/pta/data/loaders.py`.

**market.csv**: `ts_us, bid, ask, mid`
- `ts_us`: int64 microseconds, strictly increasing.
- `mid`: float. The loader reads it from the file; `market_from_quotes` computes it as `(bid + ask) / 2`.

**fills.csv**: `ts_us, side, px, qty, fee`
- `ts_us`: int64 microseconds, non-decreasing. Must fall inside the market time range.
- `side`: +1 for buy, -1 for sell.
- `px`: fill price.
- `qty`: positive.
- `fee`: a cost when positive and a rebate when negative.

Running on real quotes: `market_from_quotes(df, ts_col, bid_col, ask_col, ts_unit="us")` builds a market frame from any best-bid/best-ask table. `ts_unit` is `ns`, `us` or `ms`, duplicate timestamps keep the last quote, and you supply the column names. No vendor file layout has been verified. The strategy module also accepts `flow=None` to replay on any market data, which is useful for plumbing only.

## Limitations and next steps

- **Synthetic data only.** The market and the flow signal are generated, so the results describe the tooling and not a real strategy.
- **Single instrument.**
- **No queue position or latency.** Fill generation does not model the order book queue or message delays.
- **Fills are assumed to be stamped on market timestamps.** The inventory identity used in the tests (`inventory_pnl_on_grid`) holds only under that assumption.
- **Real quotes are not yet tested.** The `market_from_quotes` path has not been checked against any vendor file layout.
- **Project 2 will consume these outputs** (the fills, market and attribution outputs written by this project).
